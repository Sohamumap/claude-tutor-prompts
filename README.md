# Six prompts that make Claude your tutor

You commented TUTOR. Here are all six on one page. Each has [BRACKETS]: replace them with
your own skill, hours or text, paste the whole prompt into a new Claude chat, and answer
what it asks. Run them one skill at a time. Start with the time budget, use the blind spot
test when you think you are done.

## 1. The time budget

```
I have [HOURS] hours in total to get usable at [SKILL], starting now. I know [WHAT I ALREADY KNOW] and nothing else.

Plan those hours for me. Split them into blocks, say what each block covers, and for every block name one thing I will be able to do at the end of it. Cut anything a beginner can leave for later, and tell me what you cut and why.

Then start block one. Teach it in short steps, give me one small task after each step, and wait for my answer before you go on.
```

## 2. Break it first

```
I am learning [SKILL]. Before you teach me anything about [TOPIC], give me a short, realistic task where beginners usually get it wrong, and let me attempt it. No hints.

When I answer, do not correct me straight away. Tell me what my answer assumes, then ask me one question that makes me see the mistake myself.

Show me the right way only after my second try, and name the rule I missed. Then give me a new task on the same rule, a little harder, until I get two in a row right.
```

## 3. One idea at a time

```
I am stuck on the text below. Do not summarise it and do not explain all of it.

Find the single idea the rest depends on and explain only that, in words a [MY LEVEL, e.g. first year of college] would follow, with one example from everyday life.

Then check me: ask me to say that idea back in my own words. If I get it wrong, explain it a different way. Once I have it, move to the next idea the text needs, one at a time, same method.

[PASTE THE TEXT HERE]
```

## 4. One result, 7 days

```
In 7 days I want to be able to [ONE CONCRETE RESULT, e.g. ship a working login page]. I can give it [MINUTES] minutes a day. I already know [WHAT I KNOW].

Work backwards from that result. List what I must be able to do on day 7, then spread it over the 7 days so each day ends with something I can show. For each day give me the task, how I check it worked, and the one thing beginners waste time on that day. Skip any theory this result does not need.

Each morning I will tell you how yesterday went. Adjust the plan from there.
```

## 5. The blind spot test

```
I have been using [SKILL] for [HOW LONG] and I think my basics are fine. Test that.

Give me 6 short questions, one at a time, starting easy and getting harder. Each one should look simple and only work if I really understand [SKILL]. Wait for my answer every time.

After each answer, tell me in one line whether it was right and what it shows about my understanding. At the end, list the gaps you found, rank them by how much they would hurt me in real work, and give me one exercise for the biggest one.
```

## 6. Teach it back

```
I just learned [TOPIC]. I am going to teach it to you as if you know nothing about it, and you will play a curious beginner.

Ask the questions a beginner would ask: why does that happen, what if I do this instead, what does that word mean. Whenever I say something vague or wrong, or skip a step, ask me to explain that part again instead of filling it in for me.

After I finish, drop the act. Tell me which parts I explained clearly and which parts I was hiding behind words I do not fully understand.
```

Two habits that make all six work better: answer in full sentences, so it has something to
check, and when it skips the wait and answers for you, reply "you did not wait, ask me
again" and it will.
